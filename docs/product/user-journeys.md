# Industrial Learn User Journeys

## Core User Journeys

Industrial Learn has five core journeys:

- Student learning journey.
- Lecturer journey.
- Content-author journey.
- Engineering-review journey.
- Project and professional development journey.

Each journey must support the two connected schools: Core Engineering and Future Engineering.

## Student Learning Journey

1. Student signs in and chooses Core Engineering or Future Engineering.
2. Student selects a topic based on course, skill gap, assignment, or interest.
3. Student sees the topic status, learning outcomes, prerequisites, and source-backed content state.
4. Student studies a concept explanation.
5. Student works through a guided calculation with units, assumptions, and feedback.
6. Student runs an interactive simulation to test cause and effect.
7. Student attempts normal-state, boundary-state, or fault-state practice.
8. Student completes a short assessment.
9. Student receives feedback showing which concept, calculation step, or diagnostic decision needs more practice.
10. Student sees progress update across topic, skill, calculation, simulation, and project evidence.
11. Student follows a connection from Core Engineering to a related Future Engineering topic.
12. Student completes a design or project activity that requires explanation of assumptions, limitations, and decisions.

## Student Learning Journey Stages

### Stage 1: Orientation

The student understands what the topic covers, why it matters, and what prerequisite knowledge is needed.

### Stage 2: Concept Foundation

The student learns the theory with source-backed explanations and clear examples.

### Stage 3: Calculation Practice

The student completes step-by-step calculations using consistent SI units internally.

### Stage 4: Simulation Exploration

The student changes inputs and observes system behaviour in normal, boundary, and fault conditions.

### Stage 5: Diagnosis And Design

The student identifies likely faults, evaluates constraints, and justifies a design or troubleshooting decision.

### Stage 6: Assessment And Reflection

The student receives feedback and reflects on assumptions, limitations, and remaining gaps.

### Stage 7: Progression

The student advances to a connected Core Engineering or Future Engineering topic.

## Lecturer Journey

1. Lecturer signs in and selects a course or cohort.
2. Lecturer browses available reviewed modules.
3. Lecturer checks content status before assigning a module.
4. Lecturer assigns a lesson, calculation activity, simulation, assessment, or project.
5. Students complete assigned work.
6. Lecturer reviews cohort progress by topic, misconception, calculation step, assessment result, and project completion.
7. Lecturer identifies students needing support.
8. Lecturer exports or records summary evidence according to institutional policy.
9. Lecturer gives targeted class support based on common gaps.
10. Lecturer requests new content, flags an issue, or suggests a review update.

## Content-Author Journey

1. Author creates a draft topic, lesson, assessment, project, or simulation specification.
2. Author assigns the correct content status.
3. Author adds important technical statements with approved source IDs.
4. Author defines learning outcomes, prerequisites, assumptions, and limitations.
5. Author links calculation requirements to pure calculation functions rather than embedding formulas in UI content.
6. Author writes assessment feedback and project rubrics.
7. Author prepares simulation behaviour expectations for normal, boundary, and fault states.
8. Author submits the content for engineering review.
9. Author receives reviewer feedback.
10. Author revises content within the approved scope.
11. Author resubmits until the content is approved or returned for further work.

## Engineering-Review Journey

1. Reviewer opens content waiting for review.
2. Reviewer checks the content status and review request type.
3. Reviewer verifies source IDs for important technical statements.
4. Reviewer checks equations, assumptions, inputs, outputs, and SI unit handling.
5. Reviewer checks simulation expected behaviour for normal-state, boundary-state, and fault-state cases.
6. Reviewer flags unsupported claims, unsafe guidance, unclear assumptions, or incorrect calculations.
7. Reviewer approves, requests changes, or rejects the item.
8. Review outcome is recorded with reviewer identity, date, status, and notes.
9. Approved content can be labelled Approved for student use only when the review record exists.

## Project And Professional Development Journey

1. Student selects a project linked to a Core Engineering topic.
2. Student reviews constraints, assumptions, source-backed references, and required deliverables.
3. Student completes calculations and design reasoning.
4. Student tests selected design choices through simulation where available.
5. Student records professional reflection: assumptions, limitations, risks, and next steps.
6. Student submits the project.
7. Lecturer or reviewer assesses the project against a rubric.
8. Student receives feedback and progress credit toward junior practice readiness.

## Cross-School Journey

1. Student completes a Core Engineering topic.
2. The platform recommends a connected Future Engineering topic.
3. Student sees which fundamentals are reused in the future topic.
4. Student studies how the modern technology extends, automates, optimises, or changes the original engineering problem.
5. Student completes a short future-facing activity without losing sight of fundamentals, assumptions, safety, and sources.
