# Source Quality Criteria

The controlling policy is `docs/content/academic-source-quality-policy.md`.

## Authority Levels

| Level | Authority category                               | Use                                                                                             |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Governing or authoritative                       | Legislation, regulations, SANS, IEC, ISO, ASHRAE, and applicable safety requirements            |
| 2     | Official technical documentation                 | Equipment-specific manufacturer and official platform behaviour, configuration, and application |
| 3     | University engineering textbook                  | Academic theory and equations from an exact, legally obtained university-level edition          |
| 4     | University or peer-reviewed educational material | University course material, recognised open textbooks, peer-reviewed work, and established OER  |
| 5     | Supporting                                       | Supplementary explanation, illustration, planning records, and unresolved placeholders only     |

Level 1 and Level 2 sources take precedence within their governing or equipment-specific scope.
McGraw Hill engineering textbooks are a preferred Level 3 benchmark where appropriate and legally
available, but publisher preference never overrides governing requirements or official
equipment-specific documentation.

## Approval Criteria

An approved source must have:

- Stable source ID.
- Authority level and matching authority category.
- Source role, selection rationale, and preferred-academic-publisher assessment where applicable.
- Actual file or permitted metadata reference.
- Confirmed title and author or organisation.
- Confirmed edition, version or publication date when available.
- Copyright status.
- Relevant chapter, section or page metadata where cited.
- Named reviewer and review date.
- Copyright, licence, project-access, and distribution metadata.
- Traceability to supported knowledge files, equations, and simulations.
- Recorded source-conflict status.
- Evidence status set to `approved`.

Level 5 sources may be checked for supplementary use, but they must have a supporting role and must
not be the primary authority or count as corroboration for an important technical model. Placeholder
source records must never be approved evidence. Unresolved source conflicts block student-use
approval. Significant equations, simulations, design principles, safety limits, fault behaviour,
and operating limits require a second Level 1-4 source where practical, or a named independent-review
exception for the exact content version.
